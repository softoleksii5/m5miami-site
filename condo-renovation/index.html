In short

How a condo renovation in Miami works, in short

The short answer. A condo renovation in Miami follows the same order almost every time. You get your building's renovation rules first. Then the project is designed and priced. The association approves the plans. The contractor partners pull permits where the work needs them. Construction runs inside the building's work hours, and you finish with a walkthrough. In a condo, the building often sets the pace more than the work itself.

  1. Rules. Ask building management for the renovation rules and the application form before you design anything.
  2. Design and budget. Layouts, 3D, materials and an itemized estimate, 48 hours after a free site visit.
  3. Association approval. The board reviews the plans. Timing depends on the building, and some boards meet only monthly.
  4. Permits. Needed when the work touches plumbing, electrical, walls, windows or air conditioning. The contractor partner pulls them.
  5. Construction. Within the building's work hours and elevator windows, with weekly photo updates.
  6. Handover. Final cleaning, a walkthrough, a punch-list and a 1-year written warranty.

Step by step

The steps and how long each takes

  1. Building rules (a few days). Ask management for the renovation rules, the application form and the insurance requirements.
  2. Free site visit and brief (about a week). We meet at your space, measure, and check what can and cannot move.
  3. Concept (about 1–2 weeks). Two or three directions for style and layout.
  4. Design, 3D and budget (about 2–3 weeks). Layouts, 3D, materials and the itemized budget. In total the design stage takes roughly 4–6 weeks from the first consultation.
  5. Association approval (set by the building). We prepare the paperwork. Some boards review applications only at scheduled meetings, so we submit early.
  6. Permits (set by the city or county). Pulled by the contractor partner where the work needs them.
  7. Construction (per the estimate). Inside the building's work hours. Weekly photo updates and milestone payments.
  8. Handover. Final cleaning, a walkthrough, a punch-list and closing out the deposit with the building.

How long does it take overall? A single room, such as a kitchen or a bathroom, typically takes up to 3 months of work on site. A whole apartment takes longer: our completed apartment projects took from 3.5 to 7.5 months, depending on size.

Building rules

Building rules: board approval, COI, work hours and the elevator

Every building has its own rules, and they are written in your building's declaration and renovation rules. These are the things owners meet most often.

  • Board approval. For work beyond cosmetic changes, the association usually has to approve the plans before work starts. Management normally runs the application. Expect to submit plans, the contractor's details and a work schedule.
  • COI (certificate of insurance). A document from the contractor's insurer that names the association and the management company. Most buildings will not let work start without it.
  • Work hours and noise. Noisy work is limited to set hours on set days. Many buildings restrict weekends and holidays. Shorter hours mean a longer schedule, so we plan around them from day one.
  • Elevator. The service elevator is reserved in advance for materials, deliveries and debris. Common areas are protected.
  • Deposits. Many buildings ask for a deposit against damage to common areas. Amounts and refund terms vary.
  • Floors. Many buildings set sound-insulation rules for hard flooring.
  • Contractor requirements. Some buildings require a Florida-licensed, insured contractor, or keep a list of approved ones.

What you may change inside your own unit, and how to apply, is set by your building's declaration and rules. Ask management for them in writing before you plan anything.

Permits

Permits: when you need one and who pulls it

Paint and surface finishes generally do not need a permit. Moving plumbing, changing electrical circuits, opening or moving walls, replacing windows or changing air conditioning generally does. The contractor who holds the Florida license pulls the permit. In an M5 project that is our contractor partner, working under their own permit and contract. If you are unsure, the local building department can tell you.

What is a lien release, and why does it matter? In Florida, a subcontractor or supplier who has not been paid can file a construction lien against the property, even if you paid the contractor in full. A lien release is a signed statement that they have been paid. On every M5 project we collect lien releases from every subcontractor. FAQ

Questions

Do I need the association's approval to renovate my condo in Miami?

In most buildings, yes, for anything beyond cosmetic work. Every association has its own rules, so check your building's declaration and rules first. We prepare the paperwork and coordinate the building process; the exact requirements come from your association.

What can and cannot be changed in a condo renovation?

Inside your unit, finishes, kitchens, bathrooms and non-structural layouts can usually be redesigned. Structure, shared plumbing stacks and exterior elements are usually off limits or need special approval. Your association's rules and the building decide, so we check both before we design.

How long does a full condo renovation take?

It depends on the size, the scope and the building's approval process. The design stage takes roughly 4–6 weeks, from the first consultation to the design, 3D and itemized budget. The construction time follows the estimate, with weekly photo updates. You get the timeline upfront.

Who pulls the permits and deals with the building management?

We coordinate it. Permits are pulled by our licensed & insured Florida contractor partners; COI for your building, elevator bookings and noise-hour rules are part of our process, not your problem.

Can a closed condo kitchen be opened to the living room?

Sometimes. It depends on what the wall is: a simple partition can often be opened, while a structural or shared wall usually cannot. We check the building drawings and your association's rules before we design the new layout.

What is a COI, and why does my building ask for it?

A COI is a certificate of insurance from the contractor's insurer. It names the association and the management company, and most buildings require it before any work starts. We handle the request for your building together with our licensed & insured contractor partners.

What are the usual work hours in a Miami condo?

It depends on the building. Noisy work is usually limited to set hours on set days, and many buildings restrict weekends and holidays. Ask management for the written rules, and we plan the schedule around them.

Do I need to reserve the elevator?

Almost always. The service elevator is normally booked in advance for deliveries and debris removal, and common areas are protected. We book the elevator slots as part of the schedule.

How do payments work?

By milestone, as stages are completed, with no large upfront payment. The estimate is itemized and written before the contract, and it changes only through a change order that you approve first.

What is a lien release?

In Florida, an unpaid subcontractor or supplier can file a lien against the property even if you paid the contractor. A lien release is a signed statement that they have been paid. We collect lien releases from every subcontractor.
