Miami home design report 2026

For ten years the default luxury home was white walls, grey floors and a glossy kitchen. It photographed well and lived badly. This is a short field guide to what is replacing it in South Florida, and to the mistakes that are expensive to fix.

Ten trends

Ten trends worth your money

1. Warm earth palettes

Clay, sand, terracotta, olive and warm off-white are replacing cool white and icy grey. Miami light is hard and blue, and cool palettes go sterile by noon. Pick three tones, not eight, and test samples on the actual wall in the morning, at noon and in the evening.

2. Textured walls

Venetian plaster, stone and handmade tile give walls depth, not just colour. Flat painted drywall shows every flaw in strong sun. Use one textured hero wall per room, or a whole room in plaster, never a single accent patch. See Venetian plaster.

3. Medium and warm wood

Walnut, smoked oak and teak tones are replacing pale Scandinavian oak, which yellows unevenly in strong UV. Match the wood across cabinetry, doors and flooring, or contrast two species on purpose.

4. Natural stone, used as architecture

Large slabs with sweeping veining on fireplaces, backsplashes, stair treads and bath walls, not only on counters. Stone handles humidity well and reads as permanent. Book-match the slabs, and look at the actual slab instead of a sample.

5. Curves and rounded forms

Arched openings, rounded islands and soft-edged plaster niches soften the glass-box look of many new builds. Curves belong in plaster: build the radius in and finish it in mineral plaster.

6. Garden rooms and indoor-outdoor zones

A covered room that belongs to both the house and the garden, with sliding or pivot glass, the same floor level inside and out, ceiling fans and an outdoor kitchen. Plan deep overhangs, impact-rated sliders, non-slip stone and drainage before the slab is poured.

7. Wellness at home

A sauna, a cold plunge, a steam shower, an outdoor shower. Plan ventilation and drainage from day one, because a sauna added later is a sauna that rots.

8. Analog rooms

A library, a listening room, a card room: no screens, good lighting, heavy curtains. Hide the acoustic treatment in the joinery and use plaster walls for warmth.

9. Hidden technology

Screens behind joinery, speakers in the ceiling, lighting in the millwork and no visible keypads. Run conduit everywhere during framing, since pulling cable later means opening plaster.

10. Resilience built in

Impact glass, concrete structure, raised living floors, deep overhangs and repairable natural materials. Raise the finished floor, oversize the overhangs and choose materials you can repair instead of replace: plaster, stone, solid wood.

Ten mistakes

Ten mistakes that cost six figures to fix

  1. Chasing what is trending on social media instead of what you will want in ten years.
  2. Designing for the photo, not the morning. Prep space, storage and traffic flow come first.
  3. One overhead light per room. Layer it: ambient, task and accent.
  4. A rug that fits under the coffee table and nothing else. Size up.
  5. Small art on big walls.
  6. Glossy tile near water. It is a slip hazard and it reads cheap.
  7. Small windows on the side with the view. Put the big windows on the side with the view, not the neighbour.
  8. All glazing facing west. In Miami that means heat, glare and a power bill you will notice.
  9. Open shelving in a humid kitchen. It collects dust and grease fast, and salt air makes it worse.
  10. Forgetting hurricane code until the inspection. Every opening in Miami-Dade needs approved impact protection.

Rule of thumb: if a decision touches the slab, the roof or an opening, it costs ten times more to change after framing than on paper. Decide those first.
