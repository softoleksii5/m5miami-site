Venetian plaster in a Miami condo: board rules, noise, dust and timeline

Venetian plaster is one of the few upgrades you can make to a Miami condo without touching plumbing, wiring or walls. It is a surface finish, so it generally needs no permit, but it still has to fit your building: the association's rules, work hours, the service elevator and an insurance certificate. Here is what management usually asks for, which steps make noise or dust, how long a room usually takes and how we work in a tower. The finish itself is on our Venetian plaster page.

01

A wall finish, not a construction project

In Florida, permits follow the work, not the material. Moving plumbing, changing electrical circuits, opening a wall or replacing windows generally needs a permit. Finishing the surface of an existing wall, the way paint does, generally does not, and Venetian plaster, marmorino and mineral plaster belong to that second group. They go on top of the drywall or plaster you already have, in thin coats, by hand.

That makes plaster a practical way to change how an apartment feels without opening anything up. A living room feature wall, a powder room, a hallway or the wall behind a bed can be finished while the rest of the unit stays as it is.

One honest caveat: no permit does not mean no rules. Your association and building management still decide when work happens, how materials come up and what paperwork they need first. And if the plaster is part of a larger project with new plumbing or electrical, that work is done by our licensed & insured Florida contractor partners under their own permit and contract. If you are unsure what your project needs, ask your building department.

02

What the board and management usually ask for

Most Miami buildings treat a wall finish as cosmetic work, but many still want to hear about it before the first day. Some only need a notice to management; others ask for a short application, even for one room. Your building's declaration and renovation rules decide which, so ask management for them before you choose a date.

03

Noise: which steps are loud and which are quiet

Neighbors usually notice a renovation by its sound: demolition, tile saws, hammer drills. Venetian plaster has very little of that. The plaster is laid on with a steel trowel and burnished by hand, and the sound of a trowel on a wall rarely carries past the next room.

The louder part is preparation. Patching holes, sanding old repairs or removing loose paint and an old texture can mean a sander or a scraper for a while. We put those steps inside the building's noise hours and keep the quiet hand work for the rest of the day, where the rules allow.

If the wall needs real repair before any plaster goes on, that is a separate and louder job. We tell you at the site visit, before anything is scheduled with the building.

04

Dust, furniture and living at home during the work

Fine dust comes mainly from sanding during preparation, not from the plaster itself. Before we start, the floor along the work area is covered, furniture is moved away from the wall or wrapped, and the room is closed off from the rest of the apartment where possible. We clean up at the end of each day, so the corridor and the elevator stay the way the building expects.

Because the work is mostly quiet and kept to one area, owners can usually stay in the apartment while one wall or one room is finished. A whole apartment is a different conversation, and we plan the order of rooms with you.

Keep the air conditioning running during the work. Each coat dries before the next one goes on, and a steady, cool, dry room helps every coat dry evenly. How the finish behaves in Miami air after that is covered in Venetian plaster in Miami humidity.

05

How long a room usually takes

The hands-on work is shorter than the calendar. Each coat has to dry before the next, and the protective sealer comes last, so a wall is finished across several days rather than in one long day.

As a rough guide, a single feature wall or a powder room usually takes several days on site, and a full room usually about a week. Ceilings, tall walls, niches and many corners add time. Before that comes the building's side: a notice or application and an elevator booking, which in some buildings is quick and in others waits for a board meeting. Ask early.

You get the schedule in writing together with the price, fitted to your building's work hours.

FAQ

Questions

Do I need a permit for Venetian plaster in a Miami condo?

Generally no. Venetian plaster, marmorino and mineral plaster are surface finishes on existing walls, like paint. Work that moves plumbing, changes electrical or opens walls generally does need a permit, pulled by the licensed contractor. Your association's rules still apply, so check with management first.

Does the condo board have to approve Venetian plaster?

It depends on the building. Many treat a wall finish as cosmetic and only ask for a notice to management; others want a short application, a certificate of insurance and a schedule. Ask management for the renovation rules before you choose dates.

Is applying Venetian plaster noisy?

Mostly not. The plaster is laid on and burnished by hand with a trowel. The louder steps are in preparation, such as sanding old repairs, and we schedule them inside the building's noise hours.

How long does Venetian plaster take in one room?

A feature wall or a powder room usually takes several days on site, because each coat dries before the next. A full room usually takes about a week. You get the schedule in writing with the price.

Can I stay in my apartment during the work?

Usually yes, when we finish one wall or one room. The work area is closed off, floors and furniture are covered, and we clean up every day. For a whole apartment we plan the order of rooms with you.
